Bionic Jellyfish Robot Achieving Enhanced Steering Maneuverability through Asymmetric Tentacles

Abstract

With the rapid advancement of the marine industry, bionic robots have become increasingly important to underwater scientific research and engineering applications. As representative invertebrate zooplankton, jellyfish exhibit an efficient jet propulsion swimming mode that enables them to adapt to complex and ever-changing marine environments. This paper proposes a lightweight and energy-efficient bionic jellyfish robot capable of achieving high-efficiency spatial swimming and steering via asymmetric tentacles. Forward propulsion is achieved through a reciprocating cylindrical cam that swings six tentacles coated with a thick silicone membrane. Spatial steering is realized through an eccentric disc cam, which adjusts the circumferential asymmetry of the six tentacles. A compact controller based on STM32F4 is designed to receive commands wirelessly and generate the required control signals to drive three motors. Fluid simulation, integrated with a theoretical model, was performed to analyze the jet propulsion force of the jellyfish robot. The 287-g robot prototype measures 110 mm in diameter and 159 mm in height in its contraction state. It was tested under various swimming modes to verify its steering capability in three-dimensional space. Experimental results demonstrate that the bionic jellyfish robot achieves a maximum average swimming speed of 7.5 cm/s, corresponding to 0.47 body lengths per second (BL/s), placing its swimming efficiency among the best of existing bionic jellyfish robots. Furthermore, it exhibits remarkable maneuverability, with a maximum steering angle of 200° and a maximum steering velocity of 22.7 °/s. The proposed robot holds significant potential for applications in underwater signal relays, environmental monitoring, and geological investigations.

Keywords: Bionic jellyfish robot, Steering maneuverability, Enhanced efficiency, Kinematic and hydrodynamic analyses, Swimming tests.

Key Highlights

  • A novel steering method based on asymmetric tentacle motion is proposed. An eccentric disc cam regulates the eccentricity and circumferential direction of the six tentacles, enabling flexible spatial steering without relying on conventional center-of-gravity adjustment or rudder mechanisms.
  • A kinematic and propulsion-force model is established for straight-line swimming. Theoretical calculation and experimental force measurements achieve approximately 96% agreement, providing quantitative support for propulsion-performance analysis and optimization.
  • Kinematic analysis and computational fluid dynamics are combined to investigate the hydrodynamic characteristics of asymmetric steering, revealing how tentacle eccentricity affects vortex structures, steering torque, angular velocity, and turning performance.
  • The 287-g prototype measures 110 mm in diameter and 159 mm in height in the contracted state, achieving a maximum average swimming speed of 7.5 cm/s, a maximum steering angle of 200°, and a maximum steering velocity of 22.7 °/s.

Summary and Future Work

A 3-DOF bionic jellyfish robot is presented, distinguished as the lightest among the rigid-body jellyfish robots considered in the study. Inspired by the motion of natural jellyfish, the robot employs a bionic mechanism based on eccentric swinging of asymmetric tentacles to enhance steering capability and motion flexibility. The robot achieves a maximum steering angle of 200° and a maximum steering angular velocity of 22.7 °/s.

A new steering strategy based on eccentric adjustment of the tentacles is proposed, enabling direction control through asymmetric thrust distribution without relying on conventional center-of-gravity adjustment mechanisms or rudder structures. A kinematic and propulsion-force analysis model is established for straight-line swimming, and theoretical predictions agree with experimental force measurements by approximately 96%. Furthermore, the hydrodynamic characteristics during steering are evaluated through kinematic analysis and CFD simulation, verifying the effectiveness of eccentricity regulation for enhanced maneuverability.

Future research will focus on comprehensive three-dimensional hydrodynamic analysis of steering, advanced model-based control, multimodal sensing for improved environmental perception and autonomy, and the integration of soft intelligent materials and variable-stiffness mechanisms for smaller, smarter, and more flexible bionic jellyfish robots.
