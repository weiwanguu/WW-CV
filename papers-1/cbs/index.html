Abstract

Manipulators are critical tools for robots to perform tasks. Terrestrial and underwater robotic manipulators have attracted extensive interest from both scientists and engineers for both research and application purposes. This review systematically examines and compares the research progress and challenges of these two types of robotic manipulators across three core aspects: mechanism design, perception modalities, and control methods following a task execution closed loop. Moreover, this work elaborates on the current state of cross-domain robotic manipulators inspired by terrestrial and underwater systems, given their increasing application demands and the unique research difficulties spanning two very different media. First, the paper analyzes the distinct mechanical structural designs developed to adapt to structured terrestrial environments versus unstructured, high-pressure underwater environments, covering mechanism design, material selection, and actuation methods. Second, it explores the characteristics of their perception modalities, including visual, tactile, and visuo-tactile fusion mechanisms. Finally, the review summarizes control strategies suitable for terrestrial and underwater environments, ranging from traditional parameter-tuning algorithms to model-based and data-driven control approaches. Each comparative review of terrestrial and underwater robotic manipulators is accompanied by insights and challenges in cross-domain robotic manipulators. This review also looks ahead to research directions such as adaptive and cross-domain universal manipulation, multimodal fusion, the integration of sensing and control, as well as edge-based intelligent decision-making. Thus, this paper not only establishes a unified “structure-perception-control” analytical framework for robotic manipulators, but also discusses potential future directions of cross-domain robotic manipulators, providing a holistic cognitive map and serving as a comprehensive reference for scholars in related fields.

Keywords: Robotic Manipulator, Mechanical Structures, Perception Modalities, Control Methods, Cross-Domain Manipulation.

Review Scope

  • A systematic comparison of terrestrial robotic manipulators (TRMs) and underwater robotic manipulators (URMs) is conducted from the perspectives of mechanical structure, materials, actuation methods, perception modalities, and control strategies.
  • A unified “structure-perception-control” analytical framework is established to reveal the technical similarities, environmental differences, and developmental trends of terrestrial and underwater robotic manipulation.
  • Cross-domain robotic manipulators (CdRMs) are analyzed by integrating insights from terrestrial and underwater systems, with particular attention to cross-medium adaptive design, multimodal perception, sensing-control integration, and edge-based intelligent decision-making.

Summary and Outlook

This review systematically organizes and compares the research progress and challenges in terrestrial robotic manipulators and underwater robotic manipulators from three core dimensions: mechanical structures, perception modalities, and control methods. Future robotic manipulators are expected to integrate the advantages of terrestrial and underwater systems toward cross-medium universal operation and environmental adaptability. Structural design and actuation are moving toward rigid-flexible coupling and hybrid actuation, while perception increasingly emphasizes multimodal sensing and deep visuo-tactile integration. At the control level, integrated perception-control architectures, end-to-end learning, edge intelligence, and the combination of learning-based and model-based control are expected to play increasingly important roles in enabling reliable, adaptive, and intelligent cross-domain manipulation.
