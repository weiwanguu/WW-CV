Abstract

With the development of the global marine industry, the demand to investigate underwater environments using robots is increasing. Inspired by the unique structure and excellent swimming ability of Aurelia aurita, a miniature jellyfish-inspired robot is developed. The robot comprises a headshell, six tentacles, a primary drive component, two flywheel steering components, a lightweight foam block, a dsPIC control system, and an energy supply component. The tentacles are covered with a 0.1-mm-thick silicone membrane and modeled in the shape of an umbrella. The membrane is driven by a gear motor to mimic the contraction and expansion of the interior cavity of a jellyfish; it exhibits the swimming behavior via water jet propulsion. Three-dimensional swimming is achieved by the flywheel steering components following the conservation of angular momentum. A built-in LoRa communication module (433 MHz) enables a robust, real-time connection between the robot and a terrestrial console. The small-sized, lightweight jellyfish robot has a diameter of 110 mm and a height of 140 mm; it weighs 318 g and achieves autonomous swimming with low power consumption. The robot can be used for underwater signal relay, environmental monitoring, and geological investigations.

Index Terms: Biologically-inspired robot, embedded systems for robotic and automation, mechanism design.

Key Highlights

  • A miniature jellyfish-inspired underwater robot combining rigid mechanical components with a flexible silicone membrane is developed based on the morphology and swimming mechanism of Aurelia aurita.
  • A cylindrical cam and six-tentacle linkage mechanism reproduces periodic contraction and expansion for water-jet propulsion, while two flywheel steering modules enable three-dimensional attitude adjustment based on conservation of angular momentum.
  • The robot integrates a dsPIC control system, inertial sensing, magnetic encoders, LoRa underwater communication, and dual-series PID attitude control in a compact autonomous platform.
  • The prototype measures 110 mm in diameter and 140 mm in height, weighs 318 g, reaches a maximum average swimming speed of 5 cm/s, and achieves a maximum deflection angle of 25°.

Summary and Outlook

Inspired by the unique symmetrical body structure and excellent athletic ability of Aurelia aurita, a miniature jellyfish-inspired robot was designed and developed. Experimental results demonstrate low energy consumption of 10.5 W, with a 5 V–700 mAh nickel-cadmium battery providing approximately 20 min of operation. The robot achieves a maximum average swimming speed of 5 cm/s and a maximum deflection angle of 25°, demonstrating relatively good maneuverability. The developed robot is compact in both size and weight compared with conventional rigid jellyfish robots. Future work will focus on achieving autonomous swimming in arbitrary directions and maintaining buoyancy equilibrium at different attitudes and positions. The reserved onboard camera will also be used for underwater image acquisition and path planning, while soft intelligent materials will be further integrated to improve the miniaturization and intelligence of the robotic platform.
